01 / THE PUZZLE

When does self-care
become a requirement?

The same purchase can be narrated as independence, compensation for hard work, or proof of being a “better” woman.

READING 01 / AGENCY

“I spend the money I earned myself. Why should I need anyone’s permission?”

“我花我自己赚的钱，难道还要别人批准吗？”

WHY IT MATTERS

Buying becomes evidence of independence and the right to decide for oneself.

OUR RESEARCH QUESTIONS

RQ1How do posts frame consumption as women's autonomy and self-care?

RQ2How are self-rewards justified—and what gender expectations appear?

02 / THE LENS

Empowerment and pressure
can coexist.

Using Gill’s postfeminist sensibility, we read these posts without assuming that personal choice automatically removes social expectations.

↗AGENCY / SELF-DETERMINATION

05 / HOW WE READ

Small sample.
Careful interpretation.

A qualitative thematic approach: follow the language of the posts, distinguish explicit evidence from inference, and compare alternative narratives of self-care.

A NOTE ON TRANSPARENCY

What this research cannot claim

Posts show public self-presentation, not participants’ private motives or actual psychological empowerment.

The dataset is exploratory and not representative. Some full texts, search metadata and promotional classifications still require checking.

The workbook lists a second-coder check as unfinished. This website does not present inter-coder reliability as a completed result.

06 / THE TAKEAWAY

Care should not
need permission.

These narratives can expand women's freedom to choose while also making shopping feel like a deserved reward for productivity or self-improvement. Counterexamples show that care can also be imagined outside consumption.

The question is not whether women should buy things. It is who gets to define what makes a woman worthy of care.
